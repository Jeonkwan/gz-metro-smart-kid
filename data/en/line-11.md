# Guangzhou Metro Line 11 — The Golden Ring!

## Quick Intro

Line 11 is Guangzhou's first ring metro line — it travels in a continuous loop around the city center.
It opened on December 28, 2024, and has 31 stations, all underground.
The line is about 44.2 kilometers long and loops through five city districts.
Its color is "Sui gold" (穗金), a rich golden tone named after Guangzhou's traditional nickname.

## Story Time

Guangzhou's metro network grew like spokes on a bicycle wheel — lines pointed in and out of the city center.
This worked well for traveling into the middle of the city.
But if you needed to travel from one district to another, you had to pass through the busiest central stations.
City planners first proposed a ring line as early as 1997.
The idea was simple: build a loop so passengers could travel between districts without going through the crowded center.
Construction began around 2016, deep underground through five of Guangzhou's most important districts.
Trains crossed under the Pearl River three times during construction — one of the most challenging parts of the project.
When Line 11 opened on December 28, 2024, Guangzhou became the first city in the Greater Bay Area to have an underground ring metro line.

## Time Story

- **1997**: A ring line was first proposed in Guangzhou's metro planning documents.
- **2016**: Underground construction work began.
- **December 28, 2024**: Line 11 opened its full ring to passengers.

The golden ring now connects five districts and gives passengers a faster way to travel around the center.
Guangzhou became the eighth city in China, and the first in the Greater Bay Area, to open a circular metro line.

## Challenges Along the Way

- **Crossing the Pearl River three times**: To complete the ring, engineers had to tunnel under the Pearl River three times. Each crossing required careful planning to make sure the tunnels stayed watertight and safe.
- **Working under the busiest city areas**: The ring passes through five densely built districts. Workers had to tunnel carefully under buildings, parks, bridges, and other metro lines already carrying millions of passengers.
- **Stations connected to the national railway**: Two major stations — Guangzhou Railway Station and Guangzhou East Railway Station — needed special upgrades to link with the national rail system. This meant those stations could not fully open on opening day.
- **Managing 26 transfer connections**: Designing a ring line with 26 different transfer connections is very complex. Engineers had to make sure passengers could easily move between Line 11 and all the other metro lines it meets.

## Route Snapshot

- **Route type**: Ring line — travels in a continuous loop; there is no fixed start or end station
- **Districts**: Tianhe, Baiyun, Yuexiu, Liwan, and Haizhu
- **Full loop time**: About 75 minutes
- **Transfer value**: 26 of the 31 stations connect to other metro lines, earning Line 11 the nickname "King of Transfers"

## Important Stations for Kids

### 1) Guangzhou Railway Station (广州火车站)

This station connects the metro with the national railway, so passengers can reach many other cities in China.
The station's design is themed around Canton porcelain — the beautiful hand-painted pottery that Guangzhou has been famous for for hundreds of years.
Here you can also transfer to Lines 2 and 5, making it one of the biggest transport hubs in the city.

### 2) Pazhou Station (琶洲站)

Pazhou is located near the Canton Fair complex — one of the world's largest trade fairs, where people from across the globe come to buy and sell goods.
The station's design is themed around Cantonese folk dance, with colorful cultural decorations.
Here you can also transfer to Lines 8 and 18.

### 3) Fangcun Station (芳村站)

Fangcun is a major transfer hub in Liwan District, located near the Bai'etan arts and culture area.
Bai'etan (White Swan Lake) is a riverside area with museums, performance spaces, and parks.
At Fangcun, you can switch to Line 1 and Line 22.

## Fun Facts

- Line 11 is Guangzhou's **first ring metro line**, and the first in the **Greater Bay Area**.
- **26 out of 31 stations** are transfer stations — that is why it is called the **"King of Transfers"**.
- Trains are **8 cars long** and run **fully automatically** with no driver.
- A complete loop takes about **75 minutes**.
- The line's color is **"Sui gold"** (穗金色) — "Sui" (穗) is a traditional nickname for Guangzhou.
- Stations feature Cantonese cultural themes including opera, embroidery, lion dance, porcelain, and traditional medicine.

## Word Helper

- **Ring line**: A metro line that goes in a circle, with no beginning or end.
- **Transfer station**: A station where you can move from one metro line to another.
- **Canton Fair**: The world's largest import-export trade fair, held in Guangzhou twice a year.
- **Lingnan**: The name for the traditional culture of southern Guangdong and the area near Guangzhou.
- **Sui (穗)**: A traditional nickname for Guangzhou, based on the city's ancient rice plant emblem.
