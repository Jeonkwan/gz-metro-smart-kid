# 🔴 Foshan Metro Line 2 — The Ceramics Express!

## Quick Intro

Foshan Metro Line 2 is a red metro line in Foshan City, Guangdong Province.
Phase 1 opened on December 28, 2021, connecting Nanzhuang in the west to Guangzhou South Railway Station in the southeast.
The line runs for about 32.4 kilometers and has 17 stations.
It links three of Foshan's main districts and takes passengers all the way to one of China's biggest high-speed railway hubs.

## Story Time

Before Line 2 opened, traveling across Foshan was slow.
Three important districts — Chancheng, Nanhai, and Shunde — were far apart by road.
Buses and cars got stuck in traffic, especially during rush hour.
People also wanted an easy way to reach Guangzhou's enormous high-speed railway station to travel across China.

Planners began designing Line 2 around 2007.
Construction started in 2014 after years of planning.
Workers dug tunnels under rivers and through soft ground across Foshan.
The work was long and difficult, but on December 28, 2021, Line 2 finally opened.

Now people can ride from the quieter western parts of Foshan all the way to Guangzhou South Railway Station without changing lines.
Line 2 still matters today because it gives Foshan a fast, reliable link between its districts and the national high-speed rail network.

## Time Story

- **2007**: Early planning for Line 2 began.
- **2014**: Construction officially started.
- **2021**: Phase 1 opened on December 28, connecting Nanzhuang to Guangzhou South Railway Station.

Line 2 became Foshan's main east-west fast route.
It helped the city grow closer together and made it easier to travel to cities far away.

## Challenges Along the Way

- **Tunneling under rivers**: Parts of the line pass beneath rivers in Foshan. Digging under water is very difficult. During construction, water once broke into a tunnel section, causing a serious accident that required major repairs and stopped work for some time.
- **Keeping water out of tunnels**: The machines that dig tunnels use special seals to stop water from leaking in. Some seals failed during construction. Engineers had to design better systems to keep the tunnels dry and safe.
- **Connecting to Guangzhou South Station**: The line ends in Guangzhou, a different city. Building a connection to one of the world's busiest railway hubs required careful planning and coordination with many different teams.

## Route Snapshot

- **Start area**: Nanzhuang (南庄), in the western part of Foshan
- **End area**: Guangzhou South Railway Station (广州南站), in Panyu District, Guangzhou
- **Route role**: A main corridor linking Foshan's Chancheng, Nanhai, and Shunde districts to Guangzhou
- **Transfer value**: Connects to the Guangfo Line at Kuiqi Lu, Foshan Line 3 at Wanhua, and Guangzhou Metro Lines 2, 7, and 22 at Guangzhou South Station

## Important Stations for Kids

### 1) Shiwan (石湾)

Shiwan is a famous ceramics town with a history of over a thousand years.
The station was built with a "Mobile Museum" design, with art inspired by Foshan's pottery culture.
Children can see beautiful ceramic-style art on the walls as they pass through.

### 2) Kuiqi Lu (魁奇路)

Kuiqi Lu is a transfer station where you can switch to the Guangfo Line.
The Guangfo Line connects Foshan to Guangzhou, so this is a very busy and important stop.
It is like a crossroads that links two big cities.

### 3) Guangzhou South Railway Station (广州南站)

This is the last stop on Line 2.
It is one of the largest and busiest high-speed railway stations in the world, with trains heading to cities all across China.
Arriving here by metro means you can then board a fast train to places like Beijing, Shanghai, or Shenzhen.

## Fun Facts

- Line 2 Phase 1 is about **32.4 kilometers** long.
- It has **17 stations**.
- Trains can reach speeds of up to **100 km/h**.
- About **22.9 km** of the line is underground, and **8.3 km** runs above ground.
- The line ends at **Guangzhou South Railway Station**, one of the world's busiest high-speed railway hubs.

## Word Helper

- **Ceramics**: Objects made from clay that are shaped and baked until hard, like pots, tiles, and figures.
- **Shield tunneling**: A way to dig tunnels underground using a big round machine. The machine pushes forward and builds the tunnel walls as it goes.
- **High-speed rail**: A type of very fast train that connects cities far apart, traveling much faster than regular trains.
