# 🍈 Line 13 — The Eastern Express!

## Quick Intro

Line 13 is an east-west express metro line that connects the heart of Guangzhou to Zengcheng District in the east.
It is olive green in colour and runs completely underground.
It was the first line in Guangzhou to use extra-long 8-car trains — the biggest in the whole system.
Its top speed is 100 km/h, making it one of the fastest metro lines in the city.

## Story Time

Zengcheng is a district on the eastern edge of Guangzhou.
It is famous for its beautiful lychee orchards and its friendly communities.
But for many years, people who lived there had a big problem.
Getting from Zengcheng to the city centre meant sitting in traffic for a long time, or taking a slow bus.

City planners knew this had to change.
Guangzhou was growing fast, and Zengcheng needed a fast, reliable metro connection.
Planning for Line 13 began around 2010.
Construction was approved in 2013, and the builders got to work.

Tunnelling under the Pearl River Delta is never easy.
The ground there is made of soft, wet river mud — not solid rock.
Engineers had to reinforce the soil at every step.

After years of hard work, Phase 1 of Line 13 opened on 28 December 2017.
It ran from Yuzhu in Huangpu District all the way to Xinsha in Zengcheng — 11 stations, 25.6 kilometres, all underground.

For the first time, Zengcheng residents could ride a fast metro into the city.
The journey that once took over an hour by road now felt quick and comfortable.

Phase 2 later extended the line westward, deeper into the city.
A partial section opened on 29 September 2025, bringing the total to 15 stations.
When fully complete, Line 13 will run from Chaoyang in the west all the way to Xinsha in the east.

## Time Story

- **Around 2010**: Planning for Line 13 begins. Designers study how to connect Zengcheng to central Guangzhou.
- **2013**: Construction is officially approved and work begins.
- **28 December 2017**: Phase 1 opens — 11 stations from Yuzhu to Xinsha, all underground.
- **29 September 2025**: Phase 2 partially opens, extending the line westward into Tianhe District.
- **Future**: Construction of the remaining western sections is ongoing to complete the full route.

Line 13's story is still being written. Each new station brings more people closer to the city.

## Challenges Along the Way

- **Tunnelling through soft river mud**: The ground in the Pearl River Delta is made of soft, water-soaked soil deposited by rivers over thousands of years. When workers dig tunnels through this kind of ground, water can rush in and the walls can sink or crack. Engineers had to inject cement into the soil to make it stronger, and special machines had to move very slowly and carefully to avoid accidents.

- **Building stations big enough for the longest trains**: Line 13 was the first metro line in Guangzhou to use 8-car trains — much longer than the trains on older lines. Every single station had to be dug out extra-long and extra-wide underground. Bigger underground rooms in soft soil are harder and riskier to build.

- **Digging in cramped city spaces**: For Phase 2, workers had to dig construction shafts in the middle of dense city streets. There was very little room for machinery, equipment, and the soil that was dug up. Engineers invented special belt conveyor systems to lift soil out of tight shafts without blocking roads or disturbing neighbours.

- **Building one of the longest all-underground lines**: Phase 1 alone was 25.6 km, all underground. Running that far below the city without any above-ground sections required careful monitoring of ground movement the entire way, to protect buildings, roads, and pipes above.

## Route Snapshot

- **Start area**: Chaoyang (west, future terminus), currently Tianhe Park — Tianhe District
- **End area**: Xinsha (east) — Zengcheng District
- **Route role**: High-speed east-west connector linking Zengcheng to the city centre
- **Transfer value**: Connects to Line 5 at Yuzhu, and future connections to Lines 16 and 20 at Xintang

## Important Stations for Kids

### 1) Yuzhu (鱼珠)

Yuzhu is the main transfer station between Line 13 and Line 5.
"Yuzhu" means "fish pier" in Chinese — the area was once a busy spot along the Pearl River where fishing boats docked.
Today it is a lively urban district in Huangpu.

### 2) South Sea God Temple (南海神庙)

This station is right next to one of the oldest temples in Guangzhou!
The South Sea God Temple (南海神庙) was built in 594 AD — over 1,400 years ago — during the Sui Dynasty.
Sailors and merchants prayed here before long sea voyages along the ancient Maritime Silk Road.
Every year, the Boluo Dan Festival is held nearby, with dragon dances, lanterns, and folk performances.
It is a National Intangible Cultural Heritage event — meaning the government protects it because it is so special.

### 3) Xintang (新塘)

Xintang is the gateway station for Zengcheng District.
"Xintang" means "new pond" — the area has many ponds and waterways from its farming past.
In the future, this station will also connect to Lines 16 and 20, making it an important hub for the whole eastern part of Guangzhou.

## Fun Facts

- Line 13 was the first metro line in Guangzhou to run 8-car Type A trains — the longest trains in the whole Guangzhou Metro system.
- The trains reach a top speed of 100 km/h, making Line 13 one of the fastest metro lines in Guangzhou.
- Zengcheng is known as the "Lychee Capital" of China. The famous poet Su Dongpo (苏东坡) wrote poems praising Lingnan lychees over 1,000 years ago.
- The South Sea God Temple near Line 13 is over 1,400 years old. It was founded in 594 AD and is one of China's four famous sea god temples.

## Word Helper

- **Express line**: A metro line that runs fast over a long distance, helping people travel between city districts quickly.
- **Type A train**: The biggest and widest type of metro train in the Guangzhou system. A full 8-car Type A train can carry thousands of passengers.
- **Alluvial soil**: Soft, sandy soil that rivers have slowly deposited over thousands of years. It is not solid rock and can be tricky for building tunnels.
- **Terminus**: The last station at either end of a metro line — the place where the train stops and turns around.
- **Maritime Silk Road**: The ancient sea trade routes where sailors and merchants from China sailed out to trade silk, porcelain, and spices with other countries.
