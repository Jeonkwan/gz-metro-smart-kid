# Guangfo Line — The Line That Joins Two Cities

## Quick Intro

The Guangfo Line connects Guangzhou and Foshan, two large cities in Guangdong Province.
It opened on November 3, 2010, becoming the first intercity metro line in mainland China.
"Guangfo" combines the first characters of both city names: 广 from Guangzhou and 佛 from Foshan.
Today the line has 25 stations, runs about 39.6 kilometers, and is fully underground.

## Story Time

For a long time, Guangzhou and Foshan were two separate cities with their own roads and bus routes.
By the 2000s, both cities were growing so fast that their edges started to touch.
Workers, students, and families needed a faster way to travel between the two cities every day.
Driving or taking a bus on the crowded roads could take a very long time.

City planners on both sides wanted to build a metro line that would cross the city boundary.
No city in mainland China had ever shared a metro line with another city before.
Guangzhou and Foshan had to work together, share the costs, and build something brand new.

Construction was first proposed in 2002, but funding problems caused years of delay.
Work resumed properly in 2007, and the first section finally opened on November 3, 2010.
That opening day was historic — for the first time, passengers could board one metro train and travel between two different Chinese cities.
The line kept growing, and the full route from Xincheng Dong in Foshan to Lijiao in Guangzhou was completed on December 28, 2018.

Today, the Guangfo Line carries hundreds of thousands of passengers every day.
It remains a model for how neighboring cities can cooperate to build shared transport.

## Time Story

- **2002**: Planners first proposed building the cross-city metro line.
- **2007**: Construction resumed after funding problems were resolved.
- **2010**: The first section from Kuiqi Lu to Xilang opened on November 3.
- **2015**: The line extended westward into Guangzhou, reaching Yangang on December 28.
- **2016**: The line extended eastward into Shunde, Foshan, reaching Xincheng Dong on December 28.
- **2018**: The final section to Lijiao in Guangzhou opened on December 28, completing the full line.

The Guangfo Line grew step by step over eight years until it connected the full corridor between Foshan and Guangzhou's Haizhu district.

## Challenges Along the Way

- **Cross-city governance**: Guangzhou and Foshan are separate cities with different governments, budgets, and plans. Getting both sides to agree on routes, costs, and operations took years of careful negotiation. This was one reason construction was delayed from 2002 to 2007.
- **Underground tunneling through cities**: Workers used 16 tunnel boring machines (TBMs) to dig through varied underground conditions, including soft soil, sand, and areas near old buildings and rivers. Each tunnel section needed careful management to avoid damaging structures above.
- **Unified ticketing**: The two cities had separate transit card systems. Engineers and officials had to create a shared payment setup so riders could pay once and travel smoothly across both cities without confusion.

## Route Snapshot

- **Start area**: Xincheng Dong (新城东), in Shunde District, Foshan
- **End area**: Lijiao (沥滘), in Haizhu District, Guangzhou
- **Route role**: A cross-city connector linking Foshan's urban core to Guangzhou's southern and western districts
- **Transfer value**: Connects to Guangzhou Metro Line 1 and Line 8 at Xilang; connects to Guangzhou Metro Line 3 at Lijiao; connects to Foshan Metro Line 2 at Kuiqi Lu

## Important Stations for Kids

### 1) Zumiao (祖庙)

Zumiao means "Ancestors' Temple" in Chinese.
This station is very close to the famous Foshan Ancestors' Temple, a historic temple complex over 1,000 years old.
The temple was built to honor a northern deity and later became a place where locals celebrated, trained in martial arts, and held festivals.
It is one of the most visited historic sites in Foshan.

### 2) Xilang (西塱)

Xilang is the point where the Guangfo Line meets Guangzhou Metro Line 1, the very first metro line ever built in Guangzhou.
Passengers can step off the Guangfo Line here and continue into the heart of Guangzhou.
This station is like a bridge between two metro systems — a gate between two cities.

### 3) Lijiao (沥滘)

Lijiao is the current eastern end of the Guangfo Line in Guangzhou's Haizhu district.
Here riders can transfer to Guangzhou Metro Line 3, which goes to the airport and other major parts of the city.
It is the newest part of the Guangfo Line and only opened in 2018.

## Fun Facts

- "Guangfo" (广佛) is a nickname built from two city names: 广州 (Guangzhou) and 佛山 (Foshan).
- All 25 stations on the line are underground — not a single one is above street level.
- The Guangfo Line was the first intercity metro in mainland China, opening in 2010.
- Foshan is famous as the birthplace of Southern Chinese martial arts. Wong Fei-hung (1847–1924), the legendary kung fu master, was born in Foshan. Bruce Lee's ancestral family home is in Jun'an Town, Shunde District, which is also part of Foshan.
- The line took about 8 years to complete all its sections, from the first opening in 2010 to the last extension in 2018.

## Word Helper

- **Intercity**: Connecting or traveling between two or more different cities.
- **TBM (Tunnel Boring Machine)**: A huge round machine that digs tunnels underground, a bit like a giant mechanical worm that cuts through rock and soil.
- **Transfer station**: A station where you can switch from one metro line to a different one without going outside.
