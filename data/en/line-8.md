# 🩵 Guangzhou Metro Line 8 — The City Connector!

## Quick Intro

Line 8 runs through some of Guangzhou's most lively districts.
It connects Jiaoxin (滘心) in the northwest all the way to Wanshengwei (Transfer to Line 8 & Tram Haizhu) in the southeast.
The line was born from a big reorganization of the metro network in 2010, when the old Line 2 was split into two separate lines.
Today, Line 8 has 28 underground stations and stretches about 33.9 kilometers.

## Story Time

Before 2010, Guangzhou's metro Line 2 was a long, L-shaped route.
As the city grew and more people started using the metro, one long winding line became hard to manage.
Planners decided to split Line 2 into two simpler lines: a straighter new Line 2 and a brand-new Line 8.
On September 25, 2010, Line 8 opened for the first time as its own independent line.
Just weeks later, on November 3, the western section from Fenghuang Xincun (凤凰新村) to Changgang (昌岗) also opened.
Ten years later, in November 2020, the northern end was extended all the way to Jiaoxin (滘心).
Today, Line 8 connects Baiyun, Liwan, and Haizhu districts, passing through exhibition halls, university campuses, and busy neighborhoods.

## Time Story

- **2007**: The plan to split Line 2 and create Line 8 was officially approved.
- **2010**: On September 25, Line 8 opened as a new independent line (Changgang (昌岗) to Wanshengwei (Transfer to Line 8 & Tram Haizhu)); the western section (Fenghuang Xincun (凤凰新村) to Changgang (昌岗)) opened on November 3.
- **2020**: On November 26, the northern extension from Cultural Park (Transfer to Line 8) to Jiaoxin (滘心) opened.

Line 8 now serves as a key connector across the western and southern parts of Guangzhou.
It links daily commuters, university students, and convention visitors all in one ride.

## Challenges Along the Way

- **Splitting a working network**: Line 8 was carved out of the existing Line 2 while trains were still running every day. Redesigning track plans and building new infrastructure without disrupting passengers was a careful balancing act.
- **Running through a crowded city**: Much of the Line 8 tunnel was dug under busy streets, homes, and buildings. Engineers had to dig carefully so that nothing above ground would crack or sink.
- **Building under water-rich ground**: Haizhu District sits between branches of the Pearl River. The soil underground holds a lot of water. Builders had to seal the tunnels well to keep water from seeping in.

## Important Stations for Kids

### 1) Pazhou (琶洲)

Pazhou (琶洲) station is the gateway to the Guangzhou International Convention and Exhibition Center.
This is where the famous Canton Fair is held twice a year.
The Canton Fair brings people from all over the world to Guangzhou to buy and sell goods.
When the fair is on, Pazhou (琶洲) station becomes one of the busiest in the city.

### 2) Sun Yat-sen University (中大)

This station is named after Sun Yat-sen University (中大), one of China's most respected universities.
Sun Yat-sen was a famous leader who helped build modern China.
The university opened in 1924 and has a beautiful campus right along the Pearl River.
Students and visitors use this station to reach the campus every day.

### 3) Wanshengwei (Transfer to Line 8 & Tram Haizhu) (万胜围)

Wanshengwei (Transfer to Line 8 & Tram Haizhu) is the southeast terminus of Line 8.
Here you can transfer to Line 4, which takes you south toward Nansha and Dongguan.
You can also catch the Haizhu Tram, which loops through the creative park area.
It is a busy hub connecting different parts of the city.

## Fun Facts

- Line 8 has **28 stations**, all of which are underground.
- The line is about **33.9 kilometers** long.
- Line 8 first opened as its own separate line on **September 25, 2010**.
- A full trip from end to end takes about **61 minutes**.

## Word Helper

- **Network reorganization**: When planners change the shape of a metro system by renaming or splitting lines to make them work better.
- **Convention center**: A very large building where people from different countries come to meet and show their products.
- **Terminus**: The last station at either end of a metro line.
